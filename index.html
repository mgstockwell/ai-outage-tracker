Are outages getting worse in the AI era?

Short answer: not in how often they happen, but in how far they reach. This set of — major consumer-facing outages (2018 – Nov 2026) shows the same root causes before and after ChatGPT's launch in November 2022, mostly changes and software bugs a company made to its own systems. What's new is concentration: the single biggest Downdetector events now come from shared providers (AWS, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, banking processors), so one failure takes down banks, carriers and apps at once.

How this was built, and what it can't tell you
  • No raw Downdetector history. Downdetector sells its archive through Ookla's paid Downdetector Explorer and does not publish it. This set is hand-curated from Ookla's yearly "largest outages" reports, regulator reports (FCC, CRTC, UK FCA), company post-mortems and news coverage. Each row links its source.
  • It can't count frequency. The set covers major, widely reported outages, and recent years are much better documented (2025 alone has more entries than 2018–2021 combined). More rows in recent years mostly reflects more reporting, not more outages.
  • Report counts mix two measures. Ookla's year-end lists give the total reports for a whole event; news stories usually give the peak count at one moment. They are labelled in the table and should not be compared directly.
  • "AI era" starts with ChatGPT's public launch on Nov 30, 2022. Timing alone can't show that AI caused anything. Two real links exist: AI services such as ChatGPT are now a new kind of service that can go down, and AI workloads add power and capacity strain to data centers (a risk Uptime Institute flags).
  • Cause labels follow the company's or regulator's own explanation. "Change / config error" and "Software bug" are the company's own systems; "Third-party / vendor" means a supplier or shared provider failed (for example FIS for Capital One, Fiserv for Zelle). Where the cause was only suspected, as with the Sept 2026 AI-chatbot outage, the row says so.
  • Gaming outages (PlayStation, Xbox, Roblox) are left out to keep the focus on everyday consumer platforms.
